# Thai Lesson Quality Assurance Report

## Scope and review method

Reviewed all 10 lessons in `data/lessons.json`, their rendered lesson and quiz behavior in `js/lessons.js`, and the lesson-page guidance in `lessons.html`. Checked Thai text and translations for internal consistency, beginner suitability, vocabulary/example alignment, quiz answer indexes, quiz explanations, completion tracking, and browser speech-voice handling.

This was an editorial QA pass, not sign-off by a native Thai speaker. The romanizations are learner aids and remain subject to human review.

## Issues found and corrections made

- **No learning objectives:** Added two concise learning objectives to each lesson and rendered them with the lesson.
- **Mixed romanization conventions:** Replaced the previous mix of tone marks, hyphens, and vowel spellings with consistent plain spellings and readable word spacing. The page now explains that this guide omits tones and cannot fully represent Thai pronunciation or vowel length.
- **Overgeneralized pronoun explanation:** Updated ฉัน to note that its use depends on speaker and context, rather than labeling it simply as a common informal form.
- **Ambiguous polite-particle guidance:** Clarified the common use of ครับ after statements and questions, ค่ะ in statements, and คะ in questions for speakers who use those particles. Added a note that ค่ะ and คะ differ in tone even though the plain romanization writes both as `kha`; the patterns are common conventions, not rules for every speaker.
- **Misleading emergency expression gloss:** Changed ช่วยด้วย from “help, please” to “help!” and marked it as often urgent rather than a neutral polite request.
- **Quiz explanation romanization drift:** Updated explanations to use the same plain spellings displayed in each lesson. Rechecked every answer index and explanation against its question and lesson.
- **Speech voice list may load asynchronously:** Thai voice detection now waits briefly for `voiceschanged`, requires a Thai-language voice, and sets the utterance language to `th-TH`. Clear messages appear when speech synthesis is unsupported, no Thai voice is found, or playback fails.
- **Completion de-duplication:** Completion remains a set of lesson IDs, and a passing quiz only adds/saves a lesson when it was not already complete. Rechecking a quiz does not accumulate or duplicate score.

## Audio limitations

- Audio is generated by the visitor’s browser/device; this project contains no recorded pronunciation.
- The test browser exposed Speech Synthesis but had no installed Thai voice. The no-voice fallback was verified; actual Thai audio quality and playback were **not** verified.
- A mocked Thai voice was used only to verify that the application requests `th-TH`; this is not evidence that a real voice played.
- Romanization omits tones and does not fully encode vowel length. It should not replace a teacher, native-speaker model, or verified Thai voice.

## Items requiring native-speaker review

Before presenting the material as fully language-verified, ask a native Thai editor to review:

1. Every romanization against the spoken Thai, especially tone, vowel length, aspiration, and syllable separation.
2. The pragmatic nuance and naturalness of all example sentences in their intended situations.
3. The speaker/context guidance for ผม, ฉัน, ครับ, ค่ะ, and คะ, including how best to present inclusive, contemporary usage to learners.
4. The scope of the classifier gloss for อัน and the service-style description of รอสักครู่.
5. The implied register and naturalness of the country-of-origin question and “nice to meet you” phrase in casual introductions.

No credentials, speaker recordings, or native-speaker review have been represented as verified.
